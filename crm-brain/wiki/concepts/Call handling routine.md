# Call handling routine

The sequence every call in [[Call transcriptions]] follows. It is the spoken twin of the [[Standard intro message]] - the same two questions, asked out loud instead of typed.

## The sequence

1. **Identify and claim the inquiry.** Name, company, and the reason for the call: the inquiry has been assigned to this advisor.
2. **Ask the two qualifying questions.** Either "are you looking for a plan with a specific hospital, or a plan within a specific budget?" - or the mirror image, "a specific budget, or specific coverage at clinics and hospitals?" The answer decides whether a comparison is built from **network** or from **price**.
3. **Establish what is already in hand.** On renewal calls: when the current policy expires, and whether a visa type constrains the insurer. On fresh calls: whether the customer already holds insurance, which changes the premium for some insurers.
4. **Explain, don't quote.** Walk the quotation sheet term by term ([[Explaining co-pay and plan terms]]) before any number is discussed.
5. **Recommend once, and give the reason.** Budget plan if the wait time is the only difference; group plan over individual; faster-approving plan if the customer will pay for it ([[Handling the price objection]]).
6. **Close on a commitment to send.** The closing line of every call in the note is a promise to send something - policy wordings, a comparison, a corrected premium table, a turnaround time.

## Why the routine works
- **One opener, two branches.** The budget-or-hospital question is asked before any product talk, so the advisor never presents a plan the customer cannot use. It is the same discipline as [[Customer categories]]: visa emirate and salary band decide what can be offered.
- **The customer is never left to read a quotation alone.** The two-quotation call ends with "send me both policy documents so I can read them, then I will call you back" - the routine assumes the customer will have follow-up questions.
- **Every call ends with an outbound artefact.** That is what makes the sales measurable: a quote, a policy wording, a premium table. It is also why the next problem is always about sending it ([[Sales operations issues]]).

## Where it breaks
- The routine has **no step for a lead who does not answer**. There is no voicemail script, no second-attempt rule and no diary slot in the note. The [[WhatsApp analysis May-June 2026]] shows the same gap on the written side: a large batch of intros with no reply.
- The closing step is a promise to send, so the whole routine depends on a second channel working. [[Sales operations issues]] records unsent drafts and pending payment links as the recurring failure.
