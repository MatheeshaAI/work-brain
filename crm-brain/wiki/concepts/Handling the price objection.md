# Handling the price objection

The price objection is the dominant one in [[Call transcriptions]]. Two different price problems appear, and they get two different answers.

## Problem 1 - "the other quote is the same but cheaper"

**The reframe:** the two quotations are not different products, they are the **same benefit sheet from two different network operators**. The price differs because the operator differs, not because the cover differs. The recommendation then moves from benefit comparison to **operator comparison**: claims approval speed, service, and whether the customer can track claims in the insurer's app.

**The evidence used:** a specific, small, concrete one - a diagnostic test sitting in a clinic waiting for insurer approval, the customer getting impatient. The stated gap is 10-20 minutes, later restated as 5-10.

**The honest close:** the advisor tells the customer he would not pay about AED 2,000 more for ten minutes, and hands the decision back with a clear statement of what the customer gives up (wait time, not cover). That is the move that ends the call without a hard close - the customer takes the budget plan and says they will read the policy wordings.

## Problem 2 - "is the lower co-pay worth the extra AED 700"

**The method:** break-even, not reassurance. A typical 20%-co-pay visit is costed out loud (consultation plus two blood tests plus medicine, 20% on each) at roughly **AED 270-300 per visit**. The 0% option costs about **AED 700** more, so **three visits** pay for the upgrade. The advisor then names the uncertainty plainly - nobody knows how often the household will visit a doctor.

**What makes it work:** the calculation uses the customer's own scenario (a family that had visited twice that year and had bought medication), so the number is theirs, not the desk's.

## The patterns worth keeping
- **Compare operators, not benefits.** Benefit sheets look alike; the difference is who runs the network.
- **Always give the budget option.** In both calls the cheaper plan is presented as a legitimate choice, not a fallback. The customer is trusted to trade wait time for money.
- **Use the customer's own frequency of visits** to price the trade-off, and let the uncertainty stay visible.
- **Anchor the extra spend in days, not percentages.** Ten minutes of approval delay, three visits, two documents.
