# Open tasks

The task list the desk keeps outside the CRM - four items, each one line, each naming a client and what is needed. Themes only; the source carries identities.

## What is on it
| Theme | Task | Where it stalls |
|---|---|---|
| E-cards | Client is waiting for the e-cards of the policy | Issued policy, undelivered document |
| CRM submission | Documents received, not yet submitted in IMCRM | Desk action, then policy documents can be shared |
| Underwriting refer-back | QA returned the quote; underwriter approval needed because a prior-year loading case had been applied | Waiting on the client for last year's history and the insurer's underwriter |
| Re-quotation | Client is asking for quotations; the previous conversation has to be found first | Internal retrieval |

## Three observations
**Two of four are post-sale, not pre-sale.** E-cards and CRM submission both happen after the policy exists. The client-facing promise has been made and the work sits in the desk's hands.

**One is a data problem wearing a service problem's clothes.** The refer-back item looks like a service chase but is actually a **history problem**: the quote needs underwriter approval because the previous year carried a loading, and the only way to establish the prior position is to ask the client and the previous insurer. [[Call handling routine]] treats "did you have cover last year, and with whom" as a standard question for exactly this reason.

**One is pure retrieval.** The advisor's own note - "look into the last conversation" - is the CRM hygiene problem in miniature: the information exists, but finding it is a task in itself. See [[CRM hygiene]] and [[WhatsApp follow-up gaps]].

## Why this list is worth keeping as a concept
The CRM has statuses; this list has **tasks**. They are not the same thing, and the desk works from both. A lead at "Quoted" in the CRM can be carrying one of these four tasks. The gap between the two views is why [[Sales operations issues]] keeps recurring, and why [[Process inbox workflow]] exists to force the two to agree.

## What a good task list would add
- an owner and a date (the list has neither),
- a link to the lead record,
- a state that matches the CRM vocabulary rather than inventing a parallel one.
