# Plan delivery and review request

The email that closes a sale: the client receives the policy documents and is asked for a Google review in the same message.

## What it does
- **Subject**: `[Client Name] - Policy Documents`.
- **Body**: points to the attached documents for the recipient's policy - "your", "your mother's", "your family's" are all handled by the same template - and offers further help.
- **Attachments**: policy bond, e-card, insurance certificate, and any other relevant documents. The same set the booking SOP uploads to the CRM, so the client holds exactly what the issuer was given.
- **The ask**: a quick Google review, with the review link embedded. Mentioning the adviser's name is explicitly optional, and the client is invited to send a message afterwards so the adviser can thank them.

## Why the review request rides along with delivery
It is the only moment the client is guaranteed to feel the service was smooth - the policy has just arrived and is now in their hands. The template makes the ask cheap (a minute, optional name) rather than pushy, which is also why it reads as a courtesy instead of a review campaign.

## When it can be sent
Only **after the policy is booked and the documents are ready to share**. Where the payment was a bank transfer, the documents are issued a business day after payment - so the delivery email is dated after the payment date, never on it. See [[Takaful Emarat underwriting notes]] and [[Policy booking procedure]].

## Practical gaps
- The review URL in the file is a long search-engine link with tracking parameters; the workspace convention is to substitute a clean review-link placeholder before sending. Check it every time.
- The template says nothing about **acknowledging receipt** or about what to do if the client disputes a detail in the certificate - that falls back to the general objection handling in [[Customer objections]].
