# Info request is not a buying signal

A saved third-party sales post, not the owner's own rule. Kept in the vault because it argues for something the sales context also wants: that the reply to "send me more info" should be a question.

## The claim
A prospect who asks for more information after a pitch is usually avoiding a decision. Reps who close do not send a PDF, a deck, a case study or a pricing breakdown - they ask a question, redirect the conversation to the prospect's specific problem, and test whether the prospect is real before spending time on a proposal nobody asked for.

## The five moves in the post
1. **AI is not interest** - redirect to the prospect's specific problem with one forcing question, under two sentences, no compliance language.
2. **The qualifier flip** - agree to send something, but ask two qualifying questions first so you know what to send; under three sentences, no pushback energy.
3. **The micro pitch** - a three-sentence summary of what you do, who it is for and why they should care, ending in one specific question; no attachments, no links.
4. **The call-back hook** - a follow-up that references what was sent, asks which part stood out and moves toward booking a call.
5. **The truth test** - a polite but direct question that separates real buyers from polite declines.

## How it fits the vault
- It matches [[Lead prioritization]]: hot-lead signals are explicit buying intent, so an open-ended "send me more info" is not one.
- It matches [[Client message style]]: one clear question, concise, and a definite next step rather than a vague follow-up.
- It matches the owner's stated intent to move qualified leads toward quote, confirmation and payment - see [[Business Goals]].

## Caveats
- Third-party marketing content with an obvious pitch of its own; the five prompts are written for a generic sales motion, not insurance.
- The post is not part of the owner's own operating rules, and nothing in the vault shows it being applied.
- The claim "send me more info is not a buying signal" is stated as universal; in practice a request for a specific document can be genuine.
