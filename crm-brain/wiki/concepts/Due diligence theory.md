# Due diligence theory

A sales process change: move the buyer's due-diligence questions to before the pitch, and only sell to prospects who are already close to convinced. From [[Cameron Shaw]]'s reel, summarised in [[Due Diligence Theory - Cameron Shaw]].

## The core claim
"People hate to be sold, but people love to buy." A prospect is not refusing because the offer is wrong; they are refusing because they are not yet sure it fits. So the 10/10 frame: only sell to someone already at least 8 out of 10 convinced. Below 8, the job is to add genuine value until they get to 10, not to apply pressure, handle objections with tricks, or offer a discount to force a yes.

## The move
Open a dedicated due-diligence segment before the pitch. Hand over control: the prospect asks every question about the company, the policies, the warranty and the person they are dealing with. The pitch then lands on ground the prospect has already checked.

The reel's own framing of the $40k-a-month-by-21 claim is a marketing claim and is not verified.

## How the call changes
Open -> due diligence -> pitch -> close, with the due-diligence segment sitting between the open and the pitch deliberately. The first conversation stops being a demo and starts being a fit check. That also changes who the call is for: below 8/10 the prospect is a disqualification, not a conversion, so qualification moves upstream.

## Status and caveats
Everything here comes from one short reel, and the numbers in it (engagement, revenue) are the creator's. The method is untested in this vault, and it trades conversion volume for fit quality, which only pays off if the pipeline can be refilled. It also risks reading as a stalling tactic if the due-diligence segment is used to avoid a real answer.
