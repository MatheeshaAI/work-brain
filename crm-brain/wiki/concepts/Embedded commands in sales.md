# Embedded commands in sales

A named persuasion technique kept in the vault as reference. It comes from a third-party video, not from the owner's own method.

## The technique
Slip a presupposition into a sentence: assume something is already happening and put only a small condition on it. The video's example is "I don't want you to fall in love with me too quickly" - the sentence never asserts love, but the listener accepts the frame and treats it as a matter of timing.

The video's claim is that an assumption buried inside a sentence is accepted almost automatically, while rejecting it takes more mental effort, which is why some people are described as magnetic in business.

## The sales example given
"I don't want you to decide this offer is perfect for you before the end of the call" - a presupposition of suitability plus a timing condition. A second variant: "don't fall in love with this program before we've gone through everything".

## Caveats
- Persuasion technique, not insurance knowledge, and not part of any operating rule in the vault.
- The video states its mechanism as a fact about the brain; the framing is a claim from the source, not a verified finding.
- No owner-side rule exists for when or whether to use it, so nothing licenses its application to clients.
