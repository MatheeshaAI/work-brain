## What the run covers
The audit checks two communication channels plus the renewal cycle, then emits a ranked action list:
1. **P0 critical** - act within hours.
2. **P1 urgent** - reply today.
3. **Renewal pipeline** - every policy sorted by days remaining.
4. **Other Gmail items** - admin, product and IT mail.
5. **Priority action list** - a merged, ranked table with a source column (insurer email, CRM alert, internal email, system error).
6. **System notes** - channel health, last and next run, and the four files written.

## What was found
- **3 P0 items.** Two are renewal failures: one policy expiring that day (a 1-day notice had arrived on 25 May) and one that was already past its expiry window (a 7-day notice had arrived on 17 May, so 9 days had passed). The third is a client escalation: a comparison was sent on 3 May, the client asked a specific network-coverage question, and there had been no reply for 23 days.
- **4 P1 items.** Three are CRM callback alerts - clients who went back online, changed a plan-type preference and triggered an alert, with alerts between 6 and 8 days old. One is an internal request from a colleague asking for help with a policy issuance from another insurer (5 days old).
- **7 renewals** in the pipeline, bucketed by days remaining: 1 critical (same day), 1 likely lapsed, 2 at ~7-8 days, 1 at 15 days and 2 at 30 days.
- **3 admin items**: July renewal terms circulated by the business-development team, a mandatory account-settings verification from IT, and a lead whose identity record showed three different name variants and therefore needed checking in the CRM before anyone called.

Nothing is recorded as fixed in this report. Every item is an action to take, and the two P0 renewals are the same two policies that reappear unresolved on 27, 28 and 29 May (see [[Daily sales audit]]). The one piece of "good news" style output appears on 27 May, not here.
