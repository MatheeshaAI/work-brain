# Due Diligence Theory - Cameron Shaw

Original note: [[raw/50_Reference/Due Diligence Theory - Cameron Shaw|Due Diligence Theory - Cameron Shaw]]. A transcript and summary of an Instagram reel by [[Cameron Shaw]], posted 14 April 2026, duration 2:49. It is the source for [[Due diligence theory]].

## What the reel claims
- The claim: this method is what let the creator reach $40k a month by age 21. That is a personal claim from a marketing reel and is not verified here.
- Core framing: "people hate to be sold, but people love to buy." A buyer only does not buy because they are not 10/10 convinced the product fits them.
- The 10/10 framework: only sell to someone who is already at least an 8 out of 10 convinced. Below that, the job is to get them to 10/10 by adding genuine value, not by manipulation. The reel explicitly rejects objection-handling tricks, pressure, and discounts used to push a 5/10 to a yes.
- Method: open a dedicated due-diligence step **before** the pitch, and let the prospect ask every question about you, the company and the policies. The pitch then lands on ground the prospect has already cleared.

## The scripted lines (from the transcript)
- Opening the due-diligence step: "before we actually get on to the next steps where I break down everything that we do - the pitch from A to Z - I just want to make sure that this is in full alignment ... this is the perfect car for you."
- Handing over control: "do you have any questions about our company, about our policies, about myself? We always have kind of 10 minutes set aside here at this stage ... to just make sure that prospects know about our company, they know about our warranty policies."

The reel closes with a call to comment "sales" for a free masterclass.

## How it maps onto a sales call
Open (set up the meeting) -> due-diligence segment -> pitch -> close. The due-diligence segment sits between the open and the pitch on purpose.

## Status and caveats
- The reel's numbers (137 likes, 8 comments at capture; $40k a month by 21) are the creator's, captured at a point in time. Nothing here is independently verified.
- The framing "sell only to 8/10-or-higher" implicitly filters the pipeline: prospects below 8 are disqualifying rather than persuadable, which changes the job of the first conversation.
