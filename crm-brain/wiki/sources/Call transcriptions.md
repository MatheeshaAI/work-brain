## What the note is

Five separate voice calls with health-insurance prospects, transcribed and lightly cleaned ("It has been edited to include proper punctuation" appears 5 times - the source of the artefacts below). There are no dates on the calls and no lead identifiers.

The five calls, in order:

1. **Quote comparison** - a customer with two quotations in front of them (two insurers, two network names, near-identical benefits) asking which to take.
2. **Co-payment choice** - a customer deciding between a 0% and a 20% co-payment option on the same plan.
3. **Renewal outreach** - an outbound call about an expiring policy and a golden-visa requirement.
4. **Group cover enquiry** - a business owner asking about employee cover, salary bands and a family split.
5. **Specific-hospital enquiry** - a customer who wants one named hospital and its pharmacy.

## Call 1 - the two quotations that look the same

The customer asks why two quotations look identical and which is better. The advisor's argument, in order:
- **Different network operator, not different benefits.** The quotation sheets look the same because the price differs, not the cover. The plan name on the sheet is the *network* name (e.g. Dubai Care N4, Takaful Emarat Pearl), not a product name.
- **Service and speed of approval are the real difference.** The recommended plan is described as the one with faster claim approvals and better service. The stated gap is **10-20 minutes** on a diagnostic approval, later softened to "5-10 minutes".
- **A concrete example is used:** a clinic blood test waiting for insurer approval, the customer impatient and asking what was sold to them.
- **Recommendation given:** the budget plan, because the coverage is the same and the difference is only wait time. The advisor explicitly says that if it were his own money he would not pay about AED 2,000 more for 10 minutes.
- **Closing move:** offer to send both policy wordings so the customer can read them and ask questions, then call back.

## Call 2 - the co-payment decision

A customer is "applying between the 20% and the 0%" and wants to know if the extra premium is worth it. The advisor runs a break-even:
- The 0% option costs about **AED 700** more.
- A typical visit on the 20% option: consultation plus two blood tests plus medicine, with 20% co-pay on each, works out at roughly **AED 270-300 per visit**.
- So **three visits** covers the extra premium. The advisor's framing: nobody knows what will happen, so it is a gamble on how often the household visits a doctor.
- The customer's spouse advises the cheaper plan ("he's only been to the doctors twice this year"); the customer leans back to 0% because medicine and diagnostics cost more than the consultation.
- Outcome: quotations to be sent, decision after.

## Call 3 - renewal

- The website already produced a random set of plans; the advisor asks whether the customer wants **a specific hospital** or **a specific budget**, and offers to send a comparison on WhatsApp.
- The customer's current policy expires around **11 or 15 December** (the advisor says "something like that" - the date is uncertain in the transcript).
- A renewal must work with the **golden visa**.
- The advisor asks when the customer plans to proceed so the inquiry can be prioritised.

## Call 4 - group and family cover

A business owner, with a member list, asks about employee cover:
- **Salary band drives the price.** The lowest per-head plan was quoted at about **AED 940 per person**; another rate quoted at **AED 740** applies only to members with salary at or below AED 4,000, and above AED 4,000 the higher rate applies. A third rate of **AED 600** applies to a specific network for employees (employee health fund / HN basic network). A group option came to about **AED 672 per head**.
- **Group versus individual:** individual cover costs more, because group pricing pools the risk - the advisor's line is that not all of a large employee group falls ill at the same time.
- **Family split:** the customer wants one plan for self plus child and a different (higher) plan for the spouse. The advisor says this needs checking with the insurer, because the basic employee plan and the higher plan are different products.
- **Self and spouse plus children together** was quoted at about **AED 8,223**, direct hospital access.
- **Delivery:** the advisor commits to a turnaround time after checking with the insurance company.
- Plan presentation uses the **Basic / Basic Enhanced / Enhanced** ladder with the three networks compared side by side.

## Call 5 - one specific hospital

- The customer wants **a named hospital and its pharmacy** and is relaxed about 20% co-pay as long as the amount is reasonable.
- The advisor asks **whether the customer already has insurance**, because some insurers reduce the premium when the family is already covered by one policy. The customer says yes; the advisor notes the existing policy is with a different (non-UAE) insurer.
- Outcome: quotations to be prepared and sent within 10-15 minutes.

## Terms explained on the calls

The largest single block of talk is explaining what the wording on the quotation sheet means. See [[Explaining co-pay and plan terms]].

| Term | How it was explained |
|---|---|
| Co-payment | The customer's share, not a claim. They never claim money back; they only pay the co-pay at the clinic. |
| Consultation co-payment | 20%, **capped at AED 50** per consultation. Worked example: a 350 consultation becomes 50, not 70. |
| Diagnostics | 0% on the 0% option; 20% on the 20% option. Blood test cited at 250-300, so 20% is about 50-60. |
| Medicines | 20% on the 20% option with **no cap**; 0% on the 0% option. Medicine at 500 means 100 to the customer. |
| Inpatient | 10% of the final bill. |
| Routine dental | 20%, no cap, and covered under the dental line of the quote; check-ups and tooth pain sit here. |
| Major dental | Not covered by the dental line - implants and major surgery come out of the annual limit, quoted as **AED 3,500**. |
| Physiotherapy | 15 sessions per year, and only after a doctor's consultation, a doctor's report and (where relevant) x-rays submitted to the insurer for approval. You cannot go straight to a physiotherapist. |
| Surgery | Room and board is private. |
| Maternity | Pre-natal scans, ultrasound and consultations; gynaecology covered even when not pregnant. |
| Delivery / newborn | The baby is covered for the first 30 days under the mother's policy. |
| Outpatient co-pay options | The Takaful Emarat sheet lists 0% / 10% / 20% "as per chosen outpatient co-pay" - a choice the customer makes, not a fixed benefit. |
| Network vs insurer | A quote line names a network (Dubai Care N4, Takaful Emarat Pearl); a quote can be from different insurers offering the same network. |

## Outcomes

All five calls end with the advisor taking an action, and in every case the next step is to **send something**:
1. Send both policy wordings, call back after the customer reads them.
2. Send the quotations so the co-pay choice can be made.
3. Send a comparison on WhatsApp and prioritise the inquiry.
4. Re-send the correct per-head premium as a small table, plus the family-split question to the insurer, plus a turnaround time.
5. Prepare and send the hospital-specific comparison.

Two calls end with a recommendation made (the budget plan; the group plan over individual), one with a renewal date captured, one with a plan choice still open, and one with a price pending. None of the five is recorded as closed.

## Caveats
- No dates, no durations, no lead IDs. The note cannot be tied to the [[WhatsApp analysis May-June 2026]] conversations or to the CRM.
- It is a transcription, so numbers are spoken and occasionally approximate ("something like that" for the expiry date, "about 10 minutes" for the approval gap). Treat the AED figures as the values quoted in the conversation, not official price list entries.
- Three calls contain a second speaker identified by gender only; the customer on call 2 is buying for a spouse and a family.
- The advisor's claims about one insurer approving faster than another are **sales claims**, recorded as claims on [[Takaful Emarat]] and [[Dubai Care]].
