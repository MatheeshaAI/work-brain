# Source: Health Insurance Sales Frameworks

Two identical copies of "Modern Sales Frameworks for Health Insurance Sales (UAE)". Content is method, not client data. The method is distilled in [[Sales frameworks for health insurance]].

## The problem
Four tiers, five or six Enhanced levels, five or six TPAs and three co-pays give 100+ variations ([[Plan tiers and variables]]). The task is not to show everything but to **narrow down**.

## Frameworks (in order)
1. **Consultative selling (primary):** diagnose before prescribing. Discovery questions: existing conditions or regular medication, preferred hospitals near home or work, doctor visits per year, premium versus cost-at-treatment preference, children, planned family (maternity), budget. Three or four good questions cut 50+ options to 3 to 5.
2. **Three-option rule:** present at most three options of one tier: A Basic (budget), B Enhanced (most families; pick level by hospital preference), C International (travellers, leavers). Within Enhanced show Entry / Balanced / Premium.
3. **Value framing:** not "AED 3,000 a year" but what it covers; formula: premium savings plus benefits included minus expected co-pay at usage equals real value. The worked example claims a 10% co-pay saves AED 800 a year in premium and only loses money above AED 8,000 of claims (illustrative figures).
4. **Education-based selling:** explain why co-pays exist (risk sharing), how TPA networks differ, use analogies (car insurance). Education builds trust and reduces price objections.
5. **SPIN (for objections):** Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff questions.
6. **Challenger Sale (advanced):** Teach (an unexpected insight, such as a 10% co-pay saving 30% of premium), Tailor, Take Control.

## UAE adaptation
- Customer profiles: young couple (20% co-pay, ask about children), family (0 to 10% co-pay, nearest emergency hospital), child-only (age 0 to 18 plans, conditions, paediatric network), single professional (20%), older expat 40+ (0 to 10%, pre-existing), self-employed (10 to 20%, budget).
- TPA quick reference: Nextcare strong in Dubai with a wide network; NAS strong in Abu Dhabi; eCare Northern Emirates budget options; "Medent" (sic, probably MEDNET) regional; Aafiya various. See [[TPAs and Networks Reference (Work OS)]].

## Simplified process
1 Discovery (3 to 4 questions), 2 Present (max 3 options: budget / balanced / premium), 3 Explain in simple terms, 4 Handle objections with value framing, 5 Close: "Shall I process this for you today?"

## Principles
Complexity is the broker's advantage; customers want a recommendation, not 50 plans; diagnose first; max three options; sell value not price; education builds trust.

## Discovery checklist
Tier: hospital or clinics only, international needed, importance of specific hospitals. Profile: current cover, monthly budget (the note says monthly although premiums are annual in the catalogue), family size, preferred area, visit frequency, pre-existing conditions, maternity, medication, and for child-only policies the child's age, conditions and paediatric preference.

## Flags
- Spelling "Medent" for MEDNET (also in [[UAE Health Insurance Plan Structure and Pricing]]).
- "Monthly premium" versus annual premiums in the quotes: adjust when using.
- The note's confident TPA-strength claims are generalisations; compare [[Network tiers]].
