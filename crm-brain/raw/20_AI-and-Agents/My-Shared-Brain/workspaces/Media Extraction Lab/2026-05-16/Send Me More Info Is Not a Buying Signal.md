## Caption

You sent the PDF. You sent the deck. You sent the case study. You sent the pricing breakdown.

And then? Silence. Every single time.

"Send me more info" is not a buying signal. It's the easiest brush-off in sales and you fall for it every week.

The reps who close don't send info. They ask questions. They redirect. They test whether the prospect is real before wasting a single minute building a proposal nobody asked for.

These 5 Claude prompts give you the exact words. The redirect that pulls them back in. The truth test that exposes tire kickers. The micro pitch that replaces every PDF you've ever attached.

Stop being an info vending machine. Save this post. Use one prompt on your next "send me more info" response.

## OCR Text from Images

### [Prompt 1] — Al is NOT Interest
> "My prospect just said 'send me more info' after I pitched [your offer]. Write me a word-for-word response that redirects the conversation back to their specific problem instead of sending a generic PDF. Keep it to one question that forces them to get specific. Under 2 sentences. No compliance."
>
> The rep who sends the PDF loses. The rep who asks the question wins.

### [Prompt 2] — The Qualifier Flip
> "A prospect asked me to send more info about [your offer]. Write me a response that agrees to send something — but first asks 2 quick qualifying questions so I know what to send. Make the questions feel helpful, not interrogating. Under 3 sentences. No pushback energy."
>
> If you're going to send info, at least send the right info.

### [Prompt 3] — The Micro Pitch
> "Instead of sending a long PDF or deck about [your offer], write me a 3-sentence summary I can paste directly into an email or DM that covers what I do, who it's for, and why they should care. End with one specific question that pulls them back into conversation. No attachments. No links. No fluff."
>
> Three sentences beat a 20-page deck every single time.

### [Prompt 4] — The Call Back Hook
> "I just sent info to a prospect about [your offer] and now I need to follow up. Write me a message that references what I sent, asks if one specific part stood out, and naturally moves toward booking a call. Under 3 sentences. No 'did you get a chance to review.' No begging."
>
> The info was the bait. The follow-up is the hook.

### [Prompt 5] — The Truth Test
> "My prospect said 'send me more info' about [your offer]. I think they're brushing me off. Write me a polite but direct response that tests whether they're genuinely interested or just being nice. One question that separates real buyers from polite declines. Under 2 sentences. No confrontation."
>
> Better to know now than chase a ghost for three months.
