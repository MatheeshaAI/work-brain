# Health Insurance Claims — What to Cover in Your Content

This file is a reference for writing accurate, useful claims-related posts. Keep it handy when drafting content.

---

## The 4 types of health insurance claims

### 1. Cashless (direct billing)
- Insurer pays the hospital directly
- Only works at **network hospitals**
- Requires **pre-authorization** (except emergencies)
- **Most common in the UAE** — this is what premium clients expect

### 2. Reimbursement
- You pay the hospital, then claim back from insurer
- Works at **any hospital** (in-network gets higher %)
- Takes longer (7–21 days)
- Requires full documentation

### 3. Pre-authorization
- Required for **planned admissions, surgeries, and high-cost procedures**
- The hospital submits a request to your insurer before admission
- Insurer approves or denies within 2–48 hours (depending on urgency)
- **Without pre-auth, cashless won't work** (unless it's a genuine emergency)

### 4. Emergency claims
- Go to nearest hospital, call insurer within 24–48 hours
- Retrospective pre-authorization
- Higher chance of reimbursement vs cashless if hospital is out-of-network

## Common reasons for claim rejection (and what to say about them)

### 1. Pre-authorization not obtained
**Why**: Client went to hospital, got admitted as outpatient procedure, and didn't call insurer first.
**Message**: "Before any planned admission, call the number on your card. One call = approval or rejection. No call = you're paying."

### 2. Non-network hospital
**Why**: Client chose a hospital outside the insurer's network.
**Message**: "Always check: 'Are you in-network for my plan?' before going to a new hospital. Even within the same group, some hospitals are covered and some aren't."

### 3. Missing documentation
**Why**: Discharge summary, bills, or prescriptions not submitted.
**Message**: "At discharge, collect 3 things: the discharge summary, the itemized bill, and your prescriptions. Keep them before you leave the counter."

### 4. Treatment not covered
**Why**: The specific treatment or procedure is excluded from the policy.
**Message**: "Not everything is covered. Cosmetic surgery, experimental treatments, and some dental procedures are common exclusions. Read your 'what's not covered' section before you assume."

### 5. Sublimits exceeded
**Why**: Room rent capped at 500 AED/day but client chose a 1,000 AED room.
**Message**: "Your policy might have sub-limits: room rent, ICU per day, doctor fees per visit. If you exceed them, you pay the difference out of pocket."

### 6. Waiting period not completed
**Why**: Client filed a claim for a pre-existing condition or maternity within the waiting period.
**Message**: "Maternity typically has a 6–12 month waiting period. Pre-existing conditions: 6–24 months. Premium plans sometimes waive these."

## Accurate claim process for content

### For planned admission (cashless)
1. Doctor recommends procedure → hospital checks insurance
2. Hospital submits pre-auth request to insurer
3. Insurer approves (typically 2–24 hrs) → confirms coverage and limits
4. Client goes to hospital → shows insurance card → admission
5. Treatment done → hospital bills insurer directly
6. Client signs at discharge → any excluded amounts are paid by client
7. Done

### For emergency admission
1. Go to nearest hospital / call ambulance
2. Call insurer within 24 hours (some give 48)
3. If hospital is in-network: cashless process begins
4. If hospital is out-of-network: you may need to pay and claim reimbursement later
5. Submit documents within 14 days

### For reimbursement (any hospital)
1. Pay hospital bill in full
2. Collect: original bill, discharge summary, medical reports, prescriptions, insurance card copy
3. Submit within policy timeline (usually 14–30 days)
4. Insurer processes (7–21 days)
5. Amount credited to your account

## Key terms to explain in content

| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| **Pre-authorization** | Getting approval from your insurer *before* treatment. Like asking permission before spending. |
| **Direct billing** | Hospital sends the bill to your insurer directly. You sign, not pay. |
| **Annual limit** | The maximum your insurer will pay in one year. If your limit is 500k and your treatment costs 600k, you pay the 100k. |
| **Sub-limit** | A cap on a specific category. E.g., room rent capped at 500 AED/day. Even if your overall limit is 1M, you can't exceed 500/day for the room. |
| **Copay** | A percentage you pay per claim. E.g., 20% = you pay 20%, insurer pays 80%. |
| **Deductible** | A fixed amount you pay before insurance kicks in. E.g., 1,000 AED deductible = first 1k is on you. |
| **Waiting period** | A time you must wait before certain coverage kicks in (maternity, pre-existing). |
| **Network hospital** | A hospital that has a contract with your insurer. Cashless only works here. |
| **Exclusion** | Things the policy explicitly does not cover. Read this list carefully. |
| **Pre-existing condition** | Any health condition you had before the policy started. Coverage varies hugely by plan tier. |

## Content angles for claims

These are different angles you can build posts around:

### The vulnerable moment
"When you're standing at a hospital reception at 2 AM and they ask 'How will you be paying?' — you want to say 'My insurance.' But if you didn't do X, Y, Z earlier, that answer might not work."

### The simple fix
"90% of claim rejections come from 3 things. Here are all 3 and the one step that prevents each of them."

### The comparison
"Two families, same hospital, same procedure. One paid 0 AED. One paid 15,000 AED. The difference wasn't the plan — it was knowing what to say at admission."

### The myth buster
"'My insurance covers everything.' No. No plan covers everything. Here's what every plan actually excludes."

### The insider tip
"Insurance company employees will tell you: the fastest way to get a claim processed is having the right person submit it with the right code. Here's how to make sure that happens."

### The cautionary tale
"Client went to the best hospital in Dubai. Paid 40k AED out of pocket because he didn't check if it was in-network. His plan was 'premium.' But premium at one hospital isn't premium at all hospitals."

## Claims content checklist

Before publishing any claims-related post, verify:
- [ ] Is the process step accurate for the UAE market?
- [ ] Are the timelines realistic? (Don't say "24 hours" if it's usually 48)
- [ ] Is the example anonymized? (Never use real client names without permission)
- [ ] Does it clarify or does it scare? (Scaring without solutions loses trust)
- [ ] Does it end with a practical takeaway? (Not just "insurance is complicated")
